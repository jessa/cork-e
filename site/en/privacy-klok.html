Cork-e Klok privacy policy

The routine clock · last updated 3 November 2026

This page adds to the Cork-e privacy policy, about your account and your family. That page also says who is responsible, what your rights are and how to have everything erased.

What the clock stores

Your routines: the tasks, their duration, colours and pictures. That way you find them on all your devices, and the other parent sees the same routines.

A routine you delete is erased. We only remember that it was deleted, so your other devices know too.

For each child, the clock as it is running now: which routine or timer, when it was started or paused, what is checked off and whether someone pressed Klaar! (done). That way every device of your family that is set to that child shows the same clock. We keep only the latest state, no history, and use it for nothing else (not for the counting below either). A device set to Geen kind (no child) keeps its clock on that device. If you remove a child, we erase its clock.

Your own pictures that you add in the clock (e.g. a photo of grandma or of the swimming bag), with the name you give them. Your device first turns each one into a square of at most 512 pixels; anything else in the file (such as where or when the photo was taken) is left out. Only your family sees them, and they are separate from the calendar's pictures. A picture you delete is erased.

Counting use

As with all tools, we count per family on which days and how often the clock was opened, and on what kind of device (see Counting use).

We also note when routines are used, so we learn whether the clock helps mostly in the morning or the evening, and at fixed or changing times. For every routine that runs on screen for at least 2 minutes, we keep: which routine (not its name or tasks), the day and the hour. If someone presses Klaar! (done), we also keep, with that same routine, that hour, how long it took (rounded to 5 minutes) and whether it was done on time (yes or no). We only keep the hour, e.g. "between 7 and 8", and it comes from our server, not from your device. Not who used the clock and not which child.

This is part of Gebruik tellen (counting use; click your name at the top right) and applies to your whole family. Without an internet connection nothing is noted or kept. If you turn it off, we erase what we had. After 12 months, or when you erase your account, we merge it into totals for all families together (per week, weekday and part of the day), no longer linked to your family.

On your device

The clock keeps a working copy of your routines and your own pictures in your browser, so it also works without internet, plus a few settings (e.g. which routine is open and which child the device shows). How far the routine or timer on screen has got (started, paused, checked off, Klaar!) also stays in your browser, so the clock carries on after a reload. That copy is removed when you sign out, and also by itself when your device hasn't been able to reach us for 30 days.

If you use the clock without signing in, your routines stay in your browser only and we receive nothing.

On the TV (Chromecast)

If you show the clock on a Chromecast, Chrome loads software from Google for it (Google Cast), and Google receives your IP address. The clock goes to the TV over your own network; the TV fetches the clock page from us. We store nothing in the process.

Why are we allowed to do this?

  • Your routines, own pictures and the clock per child: to provide the service you ask for (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).
  • Counting use, including when routines are used and finished: our legitimate interest in improving the tools (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). You can always object by turning it off; we then stop and erase what we had.

Who helps us?

Your routines and pictures are on our server at netcup GmbH in Germany. Google is only involved when you cast to a TV (see above).
